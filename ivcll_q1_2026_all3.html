Key insight: Long-distance routes (>1,500 km, railway multimodal candidates) bill ₹22,000–₹32,000/car at ~₹12–14/km. Short-distance road routes bill higher per km (₹45–110/km) because truck economics are less efficient at scale. The ₹12–14/km railway multimodal rate at 4,871 cars = ₹14.6 Cr estimated Q1 revenue. Against ₹6.02 Cr variable costs: ~50% gross contribution — but true EBITDA after rake depreciation, staff and maintenance is realistically 15–25% (consistent with dedicated auto-rail logistics industry).
Why revenue cannot be shown — and why 70% margin is wrong
Why 70% margin was wrong: Our earlier revenue estimate of ₹40k/car compared against only IR haulage + loading + unloading costs (₹12,363/car). That gives a misleading 69% "gross contribution" — it ignores rake depreciation, staff, maintenance, insurance, and office costs entirely.

The actual Maruti billing rate from DB: BECH/TL/ CNs in the delivery window show ₹27,000–₹34,000/car for 2,200–2,300 km routes. BUT — these are road truck CNs (Bechraji direct to far dealers), not railway CNs. Railway billing uses a different structure that isn't linkable per dispatch without the broken ChallanCN table.

Missing costs (not in DB): Rake EMI/depreciation (~₹1–1.5 Cr/rake/year), operations staff (₹3 Cr+/year for railway division), rake maintenance (₹50–100L/rake/year), cargo and rake insurance. A realistic fully-loaded cost is ₹2–3× the DB direct costs shown here.

Industry context: Dedicated auto-rail logistics (captive rakes, OEM contracts) typically earns 10–20% EBITDA after full costs. At that rate, and working backwards from a realistic ₹6–8 Cr full cost per quarter for 3 rakes, revenue would be ₹7–10 Cr — consistent with ₹25,000–₹35,000/car for multimodal.
