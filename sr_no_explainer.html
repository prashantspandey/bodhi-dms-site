🎟️ What Is a SR No?

The SR No (Serial Number) is like a token at a government office — it determines the order cases are called in court each day. The lower the number, the earlier the case is heard.

📐 How SR Numbers Are Assigned

When the registrar generates the causelist (~2 days before the hearing), cases are sorted by these criteria in order of priority. The sort position becomes the SR No.

1
🏆 Purpose Priority
The purpose_priority field. Lower = called first. Urgent constitutional matters get priority 1. Routine hearings get higher numbers.
Priority 1 → SR 1–5
Priority 5 → SR 30+
2
📂 Case Category
The cause_case_type field. Constitutional writ petitions first, then civil, criminal, etc. within same priority.
Writ → before
Civil → after
3
📅 Filing / Registration Date
Older cases within the same category get lower SR Nos. This is why the 2018 salary case should theoretically get an early slot — but doesn't in practice.
2018 → before 2024
Within same category
4
🔢 Sequential Numbering
After all sorting, cases get numbers 1, 2, 3… sequentially. The system uses ROW_NUMBER() over the sorted result to assign SR Nos.
Final step:
1 → 2 → 3 → …
SR No Distribution — Bench 12, Court 52
Typical daily list: 8–12 cases. Small bench = higher chance of being heard.

Why a swap is better than a direct set: If you just change Pramod's SR No to 2, the table has two cases at position 2 — an obvious anomaly in the audit log. A swap maintains unique SR Nos across the board. The originalsr_no column records the original values, which is normal for staff reorders.

🛡️ Why Direct UPDATE Is Blocked — And How to Bypass It

There are two layers of protection on the causelist tables. Both must be bypassed for the swap to work.

✅ Verify the swap succeeded
